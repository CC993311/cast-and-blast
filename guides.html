Largemouth Bass

Micropterus salmoides
Freshwater · Bass

A popular freshwater fish recognized by its large mouth and dark side stripe.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Usually greenish with a dark horizontal stripe running along the side. Its upper jaw extends beyond the rear edge of the eye. Body shape is elongated and sturdy.

Habitat

Common in warm lakes, reservoirs, ponds, and slow-moving rivers. Often associated with vegetation, submerged wood, and shaded shoreline areas.

Diet

Feeds on smaller fish, crayfish, insects, frogs, and other aquatic animals.

Behavior

Often stays near cover and may move into shallower water when conditions are favorable. Water temperature and seasonal changes influence activity.

General fishing information

Anglers commonly use soft plastic lures, spinnerbaits, crankbaits, and other artificial presentations. Fishing activity varies with weather and water conditions.

Identification tip

Compare the jaw length with a smallmouth bass. The largemouth's upper jaw extends noticeably farther behind the eye.

Interesting fact

Largemouth bass can grow to impressive sizes and are an important sport fish in many California reservoirs.

Smallmouth Bass

Micropterus dolomieu
Freshwater · Bass

A bronze-colored bass often associated with rocky and clearer water.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Usually bronze, brown, or olive with dark vertical bars or mottled markings. Its upper jaw generally does not extend beyond the middle of the eye.

Habitat

Found in lakes and rivers, especially around rocky shorelines, gravel, boulders, and current breaks.

Diet

Eats crayfish, insects, aquatic invertebrates, and smaller fish.

Behavior

Often uses rocky structure and moving water. It may move between deeper water and shallower feeding areas as conditions change.

General fishing information

Common presentations include jigs, soft plastics, small crankbaits, and other lures that imitate crayfish or baitfish.

Identification tip

Look for the bronze color, vertical markings, and shorter jaw compared with a largemouth bass.

Interesting fact

Smallmouth bass are known for their strong fighting behavior relative to their size.

Rainbow Trout

Oncorhynchus mykiss
Freshwater · Trout

Recognized by its colorful pink stripe and dark spots.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Typically has a silvery body, pink or reddish stripe along the sides, and small dark spots across the back and tail.

Habitat

Prefers cool, oxygen-rich water. Found in streams, rivers, lakes, and reservoirs.

Diet

Feeds on aquatic insects, larvae, crustaceans, and smaller fish.

Behavior

Often feeds in current seams and areas where food drifts naturally. In lakes, it may move deeper when surface water becomes warm.

General fishing information

Common fishing presentations include small spinners, spoons, flies, and bait where permitted by local regulations.

Identification tip

The pink lateral stripe and spotted tail are useful clues. Some trout colors vary with habitat and season.

Interesting fact

Steelhead are rainbow trout that migrate to the ocean and return to freshwater to reproduce.

Brown Trout

Salmo trutta
Freshwater · Trout

A trout with golden-brown coloring and distinctive dark and reddish spots.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Usually golden, olive, or brown with dark spots and often red or orange spots surrounded by lighter halos.

Habitat

Lives in cool streams, rivers, lakes, and reservoirs. Often uses pools, undercut banks, and submerged structure.

Diet

Feeds on insects, aquatic larvae, crustaceans, and smaller fish.

Behavior

Can be cautious and may spend time near cover. Feeding activity changes with light, water temperature, and food availability.

General fishing information

Common presentations include flies, small spinners, spoons, and other trout lures.

Identification tip

Look for the golden-brown body and reddish spots. Rainbow trout generally have a more noticeable pink lateral stripe.

Interesting fact

Brown trout were originally native to Europe and have been introduced to waters around the world.

Channel Catfish

Ictalurus punctatus
Freshwater · Catfish

A whiskered fish with a forked tail and often spotted sides.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Usually olive, gray, or blue-gray with a pale belly, whisker-like barbels, and a deeply forked tail. Smaller individuals often have dark spots.

Habitat

Found in lakes, reservoirs, ponds, and rivers. Uses deeper pools, channels, and areas with suitable cover.

Diet

An opportunistic feeder that eats insects, crayfish, small fish, and other available food.

Behavior

Uses its barbels and other sensory systems to locate food. Activity can increase during low-light periods.

General fishing information

Common bait choices include worms and prepared catfish baits where allowed. Fishing conditions and water temperature affect activity.

Identification tip

Look for the forked tail and curved anal fin. Compare these features with blue catfish.

Interesting fact

Catfish have excellent sensory abilities that help them find food in cloudy water.

Blue Catfish

Ictalurus furcatus
Freshwater · Catfish

A large catfish with blue-gray coloring and a forked tail.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Usually slate-blue or blue-gray with a pale underside, smooth scaleless skin, barbels, and a deeply forked tail.

Habitat

Often associated with large rivers, reservoirs, and deep channels. Also uses areas with current and submerged structure.

Diet

Eats fish, crustaceans, insects, and other available aquatic animals.

Behavior

Can travel through large water systems and may use deeper areas during certain conditions.

General fishing information

Anglers commonly use natural baits and catfish rigs. Local conditions and regulations determine what is appropriate.

Identification tip

Blue catfish generally have a straighter anal fin edge, while channel catfish have a more curved anal fin.

Interesting fact

Blue catfish can reach very large sizes and are among the largest freshwater catfish in North America.

Black Crappie

Pomoxis nigromaculatus
Freshwater · Panfish

A deep-bodied fish covered with irregular dark spots.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Usually silvery with dark irregular markings. Has a deep, compressed body and a spiny dorsal fin.

Habitat

Found in lakes, ponds, reservoirs, and slow-moving rivers. Often associated with submerged brush and aquatic vegetation.

Diet

Feeds on small fish, insects, and tiny aquatic organisms.

Behavior

Often forms groups and may gather around submerged structure. Seasonal movement can bring fish into shallower water.

General fishing information

Small jigs, soft plastics, and other small presentations are commonly used.

Identification tip

Black crappie usually have irregular dark spotting rather than the more organized vertical bars seen on white crappie.

Interesting fact

Crappie are popular panfish and are known for their schooling behavior.

Bluegill

Lepomis macrochirus
Freshwater · Panfish

A colorful sunfish with a dark spot near the rear of its dorsal fin.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Has a deep, flattened body with blue-green coloring, yellow or orange lower areas, and a dark ear flap behind the eye.

Habitat

Common in warm ponds, lakes, reservoirs, and slow-moving streams. Often lives near vegetation and shallow structure.

Diet

Feeds on insects, larvae, small crustaceans, and other tiny aquatic organisms.

Behavior

Often gathers in groups. During spawning periods, it may create visible nesting areas in shallow water.

General fishing information

Small hooks, bobbers, worms, and tiny artificial lures are commonly used for bluegill.

Identification tip

The dark ear flap and dark spot near the back of the dorsal fin are helpful identifying features.

Interesting fact

Bluegill are members of the sunfish family and are an important food source for larger fish.

Striped Bass

Morone saxatilis
Freshwater & Saltwater

A silver fish with several dark horizontal stripes along its sides.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Silvery body with several dark horizontal stripes that run from the gill area toward the tail.

Habitat

Striped bass are anadromous, meaning they can move between saltwater and freshwater. They also live in freshwater reservoirs where populations have been established.

Diet

Feeds mainly on smaller fish and other aquatic prey.

Behavior

Often travels in groups while following schools of baitfish. Movement depends on water temperature, food, and seasonal patterns.

General fishing information

Common artificial presentations include swimbaits, spoons, plugs, and other baitfish imitations.

Identification tip

The multiple dark horizontal stripes are the most recognizable feature.

Interesting fact

Striped bass are found in California waterways including parts of the Sacramento-San Joaquin Delta.

California Halibut

Paralichthys californicus
Saltwater · Flatfish

A flat-bodied fish that rests along sandy and muddy ocean bottoms.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Has a flattened oval body with both eyes on one side. The upper side is usually brown or mottled, while the underside is pale.

Habitat

Common in coastal bays, sandy areas, estuaries, and nearshore ocean habitat.

Diet

Feeds on small fish, squid, and crustaceans.

Behavior

Often lies partly buried in sand, using camouflage to blend into the bottom while waiting for prey.

General fishing information

Anglers commonly use presentations that imitate small baitfish near the bottom.

Identification tip

Its flattened body and eyes located on one side make it easy to distinguish from round-bodied fish.

Interesting fact

Young halibut begin life with an eye on each side. As they develop, one eye moves to the other side of the head.

Red Drum

Sciaenops ocellatus
Saltwater · Coastal Fish

A copper-colored coastal fish often marked with a dark spot near its tail.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Usually bronze, copper, or reddish with a pale underside. A dark spot is commonly present near the base of the tail.

Habitat

Found in coastal waters, bays, estuaries, and shallow nearshore areas, especially along the Atlantic and Gulf coasts.

Diet

Feeds on crabs, shrimp, small fish, and other bottom-dwelling animals.

Behavior

May travel in groups and forage along sandy or muddy bottoms and shallow coastal flats.

General fishing information

Common presentations include natural bait and artificial lures that resemble small crabs or baitfish.

Identification tip

Look for the copper coloring and distinctive dark tail spot.

Interesting fact

Red drum are also known as redfish and are famous for the drumming sound they can produce using muscles around their swim bladder.

Surfperch

Embiotocidae family
Saltwater · Coastal Fish

A group of coastal fish commonly found along Pacific beaches and rocky shorelines.

Explore full species guide

Identification

Surfperch vary by species, but many have deep, compressed bodies, prominent fins, and vertical bars or other markings.

Habitat

Found along the Pacific coast in surf zones, sandy beaches, rocky shorelines, kelp areas, and bays.

Diet

Depending on the species, surfperch feed on small crustaceans, worms, mollusks, and other tiny marine animals.

Behavior

Many species remain relatively close to shore and forage in areas where waves move food through the water.

General fishing information

Small natural baits and light presentations are commonly used. Species and local conditions influence what is appropriate.

Identification tip

There are many surfperch species. Body shape, markings, fin patterns, and location help narrow down the identification.

Interesting fact

Unlike many fish, surfperch give birth to live young rather than laying eggs externally.

Fishing Knowledge Center

Build your understanding of fish, water, equipment, and responsible fishing.

Getting Started

Fishing is easier to learn when you understand the basics.

  • Learn the difference between spinning and baitcasting equipment.
  • Understand hooks, weights, swivels, and bobbers.
  • Learn how to tie and check fishing knots.
  • Practice casting in a safe, open area.
  • Learn how to identify common fish species.

Understanding Fish Habitat

Different fish prefer different environments.

  • Vegetation provides cover and food.
  • Rocky areas can support crayfish and aquatic insects.
  • Deeper water can offer cooler temperatures.
  • Current carries food through rivers and streams.
  • Water clarity and temperature affect fish behavior.

Freshwater Fishing

Freshwater fishing takes place in lakes, ponds, reservoirs, rivers, and streams.

  • Bass are commonly associated with structure and cover.
  • Trout generally prefer cooler, oxygen-rich water.
  • Catfish use sensory features to locate food.
  • Panfish are often found in groups.
  • Water conditions change throughout the year.

Saltwater Fishing

Saltwater environments include beaches, bays, estuaries, and open ocean.

  • Learn the difference between tidal and non-tidal waters.
  • Understand how currents move bait and food.
  • Identify common coastal fish species.
  • Learn about sandy, rocky, and kelp habitats.
  • Check local access and fishing regulations.

Fishing Equipment

Understanding equipment helps you choose suitable gear.

  • Rods have different lengths, powers, and actions.
  • Reels come in several designs.
  • Fishing line has different strengths and materials.
  • Hooks vary in size and shape.
  • Artificial lures imitate different types of prey.

Fish Identification Tips

Always look at several features before deciding which species you have found.

  • Compare body shape and proportions.
  • Look at the mouth and jaw.
  • Check the number and shape of fins.
  • Look for spots, stripes, and other markings.
  • Consider the location and type of water.

Fishing Knots

Knots connect line to hooks, lures, and other tackle.

  • Improved clinch knot
  • Palomar knot
  • Uni knot
  • Loop knot
  • Double-uni knot

Always check that a knot is properly tied and secure before fishing.

Responsible Fishing

Protect fish populations and the places where they live.

  • Follow current fishing regulations.
  • Respect protected areas and closures.
  • Keep waterways free of litter.
  • Use appropriate fish-handling practices.
  • Respect private property and public access rules.
  • Follow local guidance for invasive species.

Fishing Vocabulary

  • Structure: Physical features such as rocks, trees, or drop-offs.
  • Cover: Objects that provide shelter for fish.
  • Current: Movement of water in a river or stream.
  • Spawning: The process of producing offspring.
  • Fry: A young fish that has recently hatched.
  • Barbel: A whisker-like sensory structure near a fish's mouth.
Important: This encyclopedia is an educational identification guide. Fish identification from illustrations is not a substitute for confirming a species in person. Fishing seasons, size limits, bag limits, closures, and permitted methods can change. Always check current official regulations for the exact water you plan to visit.
