Fishing for Beginners

A basic introduction to understanding fishing and preparing for a trip.

1. Understand Your Location

Start by learning about the water you plan to visit. Lakes, rivers, ponds, and ocean areas all have different conditions and fish species.

2. Choose Suitable Equipment

Select a rod, reel, line, and tackle that are appropriate for the type of fishing and fish you are learning about.

3. Learn Basic Fishing Terms

Learn what terms such as casting, retrieve, drag, tackle, lure, bait, and catch-and-release mean.

4. Check Local Rules

Before fishing, check license requirements, fishing seasons, size limits, bag limits, and area-specific restrictions.

5. Prepare for the Day

Check the weather, bring drinking water, sun protection, suitable clothing, and a way to keep your equipment organized.

6. Keep Learning

Pay attention to water conditions, fish behavior, and what you learn from each fishing trip.

Beginner tip: Learning to identify the fish in your local waters is a great starting point for understanding fishing.
KNOW YOUR GEAR

Fishing Equipment

Understand the main pieces of equipment used by anglers.

Fishing Rods

A fishing rod is used to cast line and help control the fishing presentation. Rods vary in length, power, and action.

  • Spinning rods
  • Casting rods
  • Fly rods
  • Telescopic rods

Fishing Reels

A reel stores fishing line and helps an angler retrieve line and manage tension.

  • Spinning reels
  • Baitcasting reels
  • Fly reels
  • Conventional reels

Fishing Line

Fishing line connects the angler's equipment to the hook or lure. Different materials have different properties.

  • Monofilament
  • Fluorocarbon
  • Braided line

Hooks

Hooks come in many shapes and sizes. The appropriate hook depends on the fishing method, bait, and target species.

  • J-hooks
  • Circle hooks
  • Treble hooks
  • Single hooks

Bait and Lures

Bait and artificial lures are used to attract fish. Different fish species respond to different presentations.

  • Natural bait
  • Soft plastic lures
  • Crankbaits
  • Spinnerbaits
  • Jigs

Other Useful Equipment

Other equipment can help organize a fishing trip and support safe, responsible fish handling.

  • Tackle box
  • Measuring ruler
  • Landing net
  • Needle-nose pliers
  • Personal flotation device when appropriate
DIFFERENT WAYS TO FISH

Fishing Techniques

Different methods are used depending on the water, species, and equipment.

Spinning

Spinning is a popular fishing style that uses a spinning reel and rod. It can be used in many freshwater and saltwater environments.

Bait Fishing

Bait fishing uses natural or prepared bait to attract fish. It is used for many species, including catfish and panfish.

Fly Fishing

Fly fishing uses specialized equipment and lightweight artificial flies. It is often associated with trout streams and rivers.

Trolling

Trolling involves presenting fishing lines from a moving boat. It is used in some lakes and saltwater environments.

Jigging

Jigging uses a weighted lure, often moved through the water with a repeated lifting and lowering motion.

Surf Fishing

Surf fishing takes place along ocean beaches. Anglers must understand changing tides, waves, and local beach rules.

INLAND WATERS

Freshwater Fishing

Learn about the different environments found in freshwater systems.

Lakes

Lakes contain different habitats, including shallow shorelines, deeper water, vegetation, rocky areas, and submerged structures.

Common species include bass, trout, crappie, catfish, bluegill, and other regional fish.

Ponds

Ponds are smaller bodies of freshwater that may contain vegetation, shallow edges, and areas of deeper water.

Depending on the location, common fish include bluegill, bass, catfish, and stocked trout.

Rivers

Rivers have flowing water. Current, depth, temperature, and underwater structure all influence fish habitat.

Fish species vary widely depending on the river and region.

Reservoirs

Reservoirs are human-made lakes created by dams. They can contain a variety of habitats and fish species.

Some reservoirs support bass, trout, catfish, crappie, and other game fish.

Water conditions change throughout the year. Temperature, rainfall, water levels, and seasonal conditions can all influence fish behavior.
COASTAL WATERS

Saltwater Fishing

Explore the different environments found along coastlines and in the ocean.

Ocean Fishing

Ocean environments contain a wide variety of fish. Conditions can change with depth, currents, water temperature, and location.

Bay Fishing

Bays are coastal areas that are partly enclosed by land. They can provide important habitat for many fish species.

Estuaries

Estuaries are places where freshwater and saltwater mix. They are productive ecosystems and important nursery habitats for many fish.

Beach Fishing

Beach fishing takes place along the shoreline. Conditions can change with tides, waves, and weather.

FISH IDENTIFICATION

Fish Species Encyclopedia

Explore freshwater and saltwater species. Open each profile to learn about identification, habitat, diet, behavior, and general fishing information.

Largemouth Bass

FRESHWATER · BASS

A popular freshwater predator recognized by its large mouth and dark side stripe.

View full species guide

Identification: Greenish body, dark horizontal stripe, and a large mouth that extends past the eye.

Habitat: Lakes, ponds, reservoirs, and slow-moving rivers with vegetation or structure.

Diet: Small fish, insects, crayfish, and other aquatic animals.

Behavior: Often associated with cover such as vegetation, submerged branches, and docks.

Fishing information: Anglers use a variety of artificial lures and natural baits. Local conditions and regulations vary.

Interesting fact: Largemouth bass are members of the sunfish family.

Smallmouth Bass

FRESHWATER · BASS

A bronze-colored bass often found around rocky areas and clear water.

View full species guide

Identification: Bronze or brown body, vertical bars, and a mouth that usually does not extend past the eye.

Habitat: Clear lakes, rocky shorelines, and rivers with moderate current.

Diet: Crayfish, insects, and smaller fish.

Behavior: Often associated with rocky structure and current breaks.

Fishing information: Commonly associated with spinning and baitcasting equipment.

Interesting fact: Smallmouth bass are often found in cooler water than largemouth bass.

Rainbow Trout

FRESHWATER · TROUT

Recognized by its colorful pink or reddish stripe along the side.

View full species guide

Identification: Silvery body, dark spots, and a pink or red lateral stripe.

Habitat: Cold lakes, streams, rivers, and stocked waters.

Diet: Aquatic insects, crustaceans, and small fish.

Behavior: Often associated with cool, oxygen-rich water.

Fishing information: Fly fishing, spinning, and other methods are used depending on local rules.

Interesting fact: Rainbow trout are native to the Pacific coast region of North America.

Brown Trout

FRESHWATER · TROUT

A trout with brown or golden coloring and distinctive spots.

View full species guide

Identification: Brown or golden body with dark spots and sometimes reddish spots surrounded by lighter rings.

Habitat: Cold rivers, streams, and lakes.

Diet: Insects, aquatic invertebrates, and smaller fish.

Behavior: Often uses deeper pools, cover, and shaded areas.

Fishing information: Fly fishing and spinning are both used in suitable waters.

Interesting fact: Brown trout are native to Europe and were introduced to many other parts of the world.

Channel Catfish

FRESHWATER · CATFISH

Recognized by its whisker-like barbels and deeply forked tail.

View full species guide

Identification: Gray or olive body, barbels around the mouth, and a forked tail.

Habitat: Rivers, reservoirs, lakes, and ponds.

Diet: Insects, crustaceans, fish, and other available food.

Behavior: Uses its barbels to locate food and can be active in different water conditions.

Fishing information: Commonly pursued using bait fishing methods.

Interesting fact: Catfish have taste receptors across much of their bodies.

Blue Catfish

FRESHWATER · CATFISH

A large catfish with a blue-gray body and deeply forked tail.

View full species guide

Identification: Blue-gray coloring, smooth skin, barbels, and a deeply forked tail.

Habitat: Large rivers, reservoirs, and connected waterways.

Diet: Fish, crustaceans, and other aquatic animals.

Behavior: Can move through large river systems and use a variety of depths.

Fishing information: Anglers use different bait fishing setups depending on local conditions.

Interesting fact: Blue catfish can grow to very large sizes.

Black Crappie

FRESHWATER · PANFISH

A popular panfish with dark speckles across its sides.

View full species guide

Identification: Silvery body with irregular dark spots and a deep, compressed shape.

Habitat: Lakes, reservoirs, ponds, and slower river sections.

Diet: Small fish, insects, and zooplankton.

Behavior: Often forms groups and may associate with submerged structure.

Fishing information: Commonly caught using small jigs and other light tackle.

Interesting fact: Crappie are part of the sunfish family.

Bluegill

FRESHWATER · SUNFISH

A small sunfish with a blue-colored area near its gill cover.

View full species guide

Identification: Deep, rounded body, blue coloring near the gill cover, and dark vertical bars.

Habitat: Ponds, lakes, reservoirs, and slow-moving rivers.

Diet: Insects, larvae, small crustaceans, and other tiny organisms.

Behavior: Often found in groups around vegetation and shallow habitat.

Fishing information: Commonly associated with light tackle and small hooks.

Interesting fact: Bluegill are one of the most widely recognized North American sunfish.

Striped Bass

FRESHWATER & SALTWATER

A powerful fish recognized by several dark horizontal stripes.

View full species guide

Identification: Silvery body with several dark horizontal stripes running along the sides.

Habitat: Coastal waters, bays, estuaries, and some freshwater reservoirs.

Diet: Smaller fish and other aquatic animals.

Behavior: Some populations migrate between freshwater and saltwater, while others live in freshwater.

Fishing information: Different regional methods are used in coastal and inland waters.

Interesting fact: Striped bass have been introduced into several inland reservoirs.

California Halibut

SALTWATER · FLATFISH

A flatfish with both eyes on one side of its body.

View full species guide

Identification: Flat body, both eyes on the upper side, and coloring that helps it blend into the seafloor.

Habitat: Sandy and muddy coastal bottoms, bays, and estuaries.

Diet: Small fish, squid, and crustaceans.

Behavior: Often rests on the bottom and blends into surrounding habitat.

Fishing information: Coastal anglers use a variety of bait and artificial presentations.

Interesting fact: Young halibut begin life with an eye on each side before one eye moves as they develop.

Red Drum

SALTWATER · DRUM

A coastal fish often recognized by a dark spot near its tail.

View full species guide

Identification: Copper or reddish body, with one or more dark spots near the tail.

Habitat: Coastal bays, estuaries, shallow flats, and nearshore waters.

Diet: Crabs, shrimp, and smaller fish.

Behavior: Often feeds near the bottom in coastal habitats.

Fishing information: Coastal anglers use a range of bait and lure presentations.

Interesting fact: Red drum are also commonly called redfish.

Surfperch

A coastal fish commonly found in Pacific shoreline environments.

View full species guide

Identification: Surfperch species vary in color and pattern, but many have deep, compressed bodies.

Habitat: Pacific coast beaches, rocky shorelines, and nearshore waters.

Diet: Small crustaceans, worms, and other invertebrates.

Behavior: Many species feed in shallow coastal areas.

Fishing information: Surfperch are commonly associated with beach fishing.

Interesting fact: There are several different surfperch species along the Pacific coast.

Common Fishing Knots

Learn what different fishing knots are commonly used for.

Improved Clinch Knot

A commonly used knot for attaching fishing line to many hooks, swivels, and lures. Its suitability depends on line material and knot tying technique.

Palomar Knot

A popular knot used to connect fishing line to hooks and other terminal tackle. It is commonly used with several types of fishing line.

Uni Knot

A versatile knot used for attaching line to tackle. It is also used in some line-to-line connections.

Loop Knot

Loop knots create a connection that allows a lure or fly to move more freely.

Always check that knots are tied correctly and securely. Knot performance can vary depending on the line material, line condition, and connection being used.
PROTECT OUR WATERS

Responsible Fishing

Healthy fisheries depend on good stewardship of fish and aquatic habitats.

Follow Fishing Regulations

Check current rules for the exact water you plan to visit. Regulations can differ by species, location, season, and fishing method.

Respect Catch Limits

Follow all size limits, bag limits, and possession limits. These rules help manage fish populations.

Protect Fish Habitat

Avoid damaging aquatic vegetation, shorelines, and sensitive habitat. Leave natural areas as you found them.

Keep Waterways Clean

Collect fishing line, hooks, packaging, and other trash. Never leave fishing equipment behind.

Handle Fish Carefully

Learn the recommended handling practices for the species and follow local rules when releasing fish.

Prevent the Spread of Invasive Species

Clean, drain, and dry boats and equipment when required. Follow local guidance for moving between waterways.

LEARN THE LANGUAGE

Fishing Glossary

Common fishing terms and what they mean.

Angler

A person who fishes.

Bait

Natural or prepared material used to attract fish.

Drag

A reel mechanism that allows line to release under tension.

Retrieve

The process of bringing fishing line and a lure back toward the angler.

Structure

Physical features in the water, such as rocks, logs, and submerged vegetation.

Current

The movement of water, especially in rivers and streams.

Topwater

A fishing presentation that takes place on or near the water's surface.

Fry

A young fish that has developed beyond the earliest larval stage.
